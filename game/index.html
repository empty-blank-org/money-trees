What this is

Every asset in Money Trees is drawn as a tree cross-section — one ring per calendar year of its price history, oldest ring at the center, most recent at the bark. This game shows you that glyph with the label stripped off and asks you to read it cold.

How to read a ring

Width is that year's growth — the log of its total return, squashed so up years swell wide and down years compress to a thin band, but every year stays visible. A fatter ring is unambiguously a better year.

Color runs a diverging scale on the year's return: red = bad year, parched tan = flat, green = good — normalized within the asset's own class, so "a great year for its kind" reads the same green whether it's a bond fund or a coin. Darkness is that year's realized volatility: calm years are light, violent years deep and dark.

Scars are the dark radial seams — each one a major drawdown episode. The seam starts at the trough (its ring, and its angular position — January at the top, clockwise through December) and radiates outward through every later ring until the price reclaimed its prior peak, where it tapers shut. A scar still open at the bark means the asset has never recovered.

What's hidden

In the real forest, asset class (crypto / equity / bonds / commodities / fx) also lives on the bark outline color and the ticker label. Both are stripped out here on purpose — otherwise the bark hue would just tell you the class before you'd read a single ring. Everything else about the glyph — every ring's width, color, darkness, and every scar — is the same honest rendering as the real site, computed from real daily prices.

Modes

Name it gives four ticker choices, weighted toward assets in the same class so you can't just guess "big wide green rings = probably a coin" and win — you have to actually read growth pace, scar timing, and steadiness. Class only just asks which of the five asset classes the tree belongs to.

Scoring

Each correct guess adds a point and extends your streak; a miss resets the streak. The reveal after every round shows the real ticker, its class, and one computed fact pulled straight from its ring data — not flavor text.
