Climate Years

← money trees

Every scar in the forest is anchored to its trough's calendar angle (Jan = top, clockwise). A market-wide shock carves scars at the same angle across many trees in the same year — a "climate year." Weather is one tree's bad year; climate is the whole forest scarring in sync.

What it is

A re-aggregation of forest/prep.py's already-computed scars (whole-history drawdown episodes past each asset's own severity gate) grouped by the calendar year their trough fell in. No new price data, no new gate — just asking "how many trees scarred this year, and did their wounds land on the same date?"

The score

breadth = scars this year ÷ trees alive this year — what fraction of the living forest got a fresh scar.

R (mean resultant length) = circular concentration of the scar trough angles, 0–1. Treat each scar's angle as a point on a clock face; R → 1 means every scar landed in the same narrow window (one dated shock), R → 0 means scars scattered evenly across the year (unrelated bad-year clustering, not one event).

climate score = breadth × R — how much of the forest scarred, and how tightly those wounds line up on the calendar. High breadth with low R (see 2022 below) means lots of damage that wasn't one synchronized event.

The analogy

Real dendrochronologists read fire scars across a forest: a scar that shows up at the same ring-position across many trees marks a single historical fire; scars scattered across different years are just individual trees having bad luck. Same logic here, with drawdown troughs standing in for fire.

Caveats

Survivor-biased universe — only assets with a live parquet file today and ≥3 full years become trees. Anything that delisted before accumulating history can't contribute a scar to any year, so early breadth is a floor, not the true rate.

The scar gate (inherited from forest/prep.py) — a drawdown only counts if it clears the asset's own vol-derived threshold (15%–50% of price) and stays underwater ≥120 days past the trough. Fast V-dips, even deep ones, never enter this dataset — breadth counts scars, not volatility.

Small-sample years — 2010–2013 have only a handful of trees; a single scar there can post a perfect R=1 with nothing else in the forest to disagree with it. Those years are shown dimmed on the timeline and excluded from the ranked headline list (flagged low_sample in the data).

Data window — the price lake here spans 2010–2026, so earlier climate years (2000, 2008) are structurally invisible; that's a window limit, not evidence those years were calm.
