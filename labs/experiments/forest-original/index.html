What this is

Every glyph is one asset rendered as a tree cross-section. It grows one ring per calendar year of its price history, oldest at the center, this year at the bark. A whole decade of an asset is legible in a single disk — and the gallery becomes a forest, where old-growth bonds (many tight pale rings) sit beside fast-grown, heavily scarred crypto (few wide dark rings). Every visual feature decodes to a real number; nothing here is decoration.

How to read it

Ring width is that year's growth — the log of its total return, squashed monotonically so up years swell wide and down years compress to a thin band, but every year stays visible. A fatter ring is unambiguously a better year.

Ring color is one uniform color per ring, like real wood, and encodes the year's return on a diverging scale: red = bad year, parched tan = flat, green = good. Both color and darkness are normalized within each asset class, so a great year for a bond fund is as green as a great year for BTC even though the raw returns are an order of magnitude apart — equities and bonds don't wash out to tan just because crypto's swings are bigger. Darkness is that year's realized volatility (calm = light, violent = deep and dark). Color and width agree on purpose — a wide green ring is a fat year twice over.

The asset class lives on the bark outline and the label — crypto amber, equity blue, bonds green, commodities rust, FX teal. Every ring is drawn as a full circle; the first and last calendar years are usually incomplete (an asset that listed mid-year, or the current year so far), and their stats are computed only over the days that exist — but the band is drawn all the way around for a clean disk.

Scars are the dark radial seams: each marks a major drawdown episode (deeper than the asset's typical annual vol — a 20% dip scars a bond fund, not BTC). The seam is widest at the trough — its ring, at the angular position of the date it bottomed (Jan at the top, clockwise through Dec), width proportional to severity — and radiates outward through every later ring until the year price reclaimed the prior peak, where it tapers shut like a healed callus. A scar still open at the bark means the asset has never recovered that high.

Use the sort buttons to reorder the forest by age, growth, scarring, or steadiness; toggle classes; type a ticker to add any of the 484 computed trees. Hover a ring for that year's numbers; click a tree for the enlarged view with year labels and a stats panel.

The physics analogy — dendrochronology

Tree-ring science reads a tree's life from its cross-section: wide rings are good growing seasons, narrow rings droughts, dark latewood marks stress, and a fire scar is wounded once but visible for years — subsequent rings grow around it until bark closes over the callus. The scars here behave the same way: the wound is the trough, and the seam stays open through every ring grown while the asset was still underwater. Crucially, a climate event leaves the same scar at the same radius across every tree in the stand — that's how dendrochronologists cross-date. Here the market's climate years do exactly that: COVID (late March 2020) and the 2022 rate shock carve scars at the same angle across the whole forest at the same ring.

Caveat

Width is a monotone squash of log-return, not a linear one — a +1400% crypto year (BTC 2017) doesn't draw 14× wider than a +100% year, or the disk would be unreadable; rank is preserved, magnitude is compressed. Scars mark only drawdown episodes deeper than the severity gate (the asset's median annual vol, clamped to 15–50%) that stayed underwater at least ~4 months — and a first-ring crash is dropped as a listing artifact; hover a ring to see that year's max drawdown regardless. First and last calendar years are usually incomplete, but every ring is drawn as a full circle anyway — stats are computed over the days present, the disk just reads cleaner. Vol is realized (backward-looking), annualized from daily moves.
