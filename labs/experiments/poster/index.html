What this is

A single asset rendered as a tree cross-section at print resolution — one ring per calendar year of its price history, oldest at the pith (center), the most recent year at the bark. Everything on the poster is computed from real daily prices; nothing is decorative.

How to read it

Ring width is that year's growth — the log of its total return, squashed monotonically so huge up years don't blow the disk apart and down years stay visible instead of vanishing to zero. This is a squash, not a linear scale: a +1400% year does not draw fourteen times wider than a +100% year — rank is preserved, magnitude is compressed.

Ring color is one uniform hue per ring, like real wood — a diverging scale on that year's return (red = bad, tan = flat, green = good), normalized within the asset's class so "a good year for a bond fund" reads exactly as green as "a good year for a crypto token," even though the raw magnitudes differ by an order of magnitude. Darkness is that year's realized volatility, also normalized within class — calm years stay light, violent years go dark.

The faint wood grain inside each ring is not random noise: its wobble amplitude is proportional to that year's realized volatility, and its exact pattern is seeded deterministically from the ticker and year — the same asset always regrows the same grain.

Scars are radial seams marking a major drawdown episode: they start at the trough date's angle (January at the top, clockwise through December) and radiate outward through every later ring until the price reclaimed its prior peak, tapering shut like a healed callus. A scar still open at the bark means the asset has never recovered. The callouts along the edge name the 2–3 deepest scars with trough date and drawdown depth.

A notch at the pith or the bark — instead of a full circle — means that year is incomplete: the ring is only drawn across the calendar months the asset actually traded (an IPO stub year, or the current year still in progress).

The bark outline color carries the asset's class: crypto amber, equity blue, bonds green, commodities rust, FX teal.

Caveats

Vol is realized (backward-looking), annualized from daily moves. Scars only mark drawdowns deeper than the asset's own severity gate (median annual vol, clamped to 15–50%), so a 20% dip scars a bond fund but not BTC. Class-normalized color means the same red is a much smaller loss for bonds than for crypto — read the numbers, not just the hue, when comparing across classes. Ring geometry uses a monotone squash (above) — compare ranks and colors across rings, not raw pixel widths.
