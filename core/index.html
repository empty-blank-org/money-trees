What this is

Every asset's tree rings, unrolled into a flat horizontal strip instead of a disk. All strips sit on the same calendar x-axis — 2020 is the exact same column in every row — so you can scan straight down a column and compare how completely different assets behaved in the same year. This is the "core sample" layout: strips of rings laid side by side on a lab bench, the way dendrochronologists and ice-core geologists line up physical cores to cross-date them.

How to read it

Each year is one cell in a strip. The colored bar inside the cell carries two numbers, identically encoded to the Forest view: height is that year's growth (a monotone squash of log total return — a taller bar is unambiguously a better year, but every year stays visible, even bad ones). Color is a diverging red→tan→green scale on the year's return, and darkness is realized volatility — both normalized within the asset's class, so "a good year for its kind" reads the same green whether it's a bond fund or BTC. A pale hatched cell means no data — the asset hadn't started trading yet that year.

The thin dark vertical notch inside a cell marks a major drawdown episode's trough date, positioned left-to-right within the year by the fraction of the year elapsed (Jan 1 = left edge, Dec 31 = right edge) — exactly the angle-to-month conversion the Forest view uses, just unrolled onto a line instead of a circle. The faint red band trailing right from the notch is the episode's time spent underwater, ending where price reclaimed its prior peak; a band that runs all the way to the strip's right edge (with a small marker) means the asset has never recovered.

A shaded column behind all strips flags a climate year: a real, live-computed quantity — the share of the currently displayed strips whose ring that year had a negative return. When that share is ≥60% (and at least 3 strips have data that year), the whole column tints faintly red-amber, so market-wide shocks pop out as aligned dark columns straight down the bench.

The analogy

In dendrochronology and ice-core work, you rarely read one core in isolation — you lay several cores side by side and slide them until matching ring patterns (a drought year, a volcanic-ash layer) line up at the same depth. That alignment is what proves the ring dating is right, and it's what reveals events no single core could show on its own. Here, sliding is unnecessary because the x-axis is already the true calendar — but the reading technique is the same: scan a column, not a row, to find the years that scarred everything at once.

Caveats

Bar height is a monotone squash of log return, not linear — rank is preserved, magnitude is compressed, exactly as in the Forest view. Color/darkness normalization is per-class, so a "good green" bar means different raw numbers for BTC versus TLT. Scars only mark drawdown episodes deeper than the asset's own severity gate (median annual vol, clamped 15–50%) that stayed underwater ≥~4 months — smaller dips don't notch (hover a cell for that year's actual max drawdown regardless). The current year is a partial ring computed only over the days elapsed so far. The climate-year metric is only as meaningful as the strips you have open — two strips agreeing isn't a "market," which is why it requires at least 3 strips with data in that year before it will shade a column; add more assets for a stronger signal.
