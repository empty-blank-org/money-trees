Money Tree Forest · methodology

How financial history becomes wood

Money Tree Forest is an interpretive visualization of historical market data. Every annual band is computed from observed daily closing prices; the organic treatment adds texture, not invented events.

Annual rings

Width
Annual log growth, mapped monotonically with a minimum width so severe down years remain visible.
Color
Annual return direction and magnitude. Wood and market palettes use separate loss and gain ramps, normalized within asset class.
Darkness and grain
Annualized realized volatility, normalized within asset class. Higher volatility produces darker, more irregular wood.
One band
One calendar year. Partial first and current years use only the observations available and are marked during inspection.

Drawdown scars

A scar is a whole-history drawdown episode. It qualifies when its depth exceeds a gate set in the asset’s own units — a fall of 1.25 times its median annual volatility, in log terms, never shallower than 10% — and it remains underwater for at least 120 days after its trough or is still open. That is about a 12% fall for a currency fund, 20% for the S&P 500 and 57% for Bitcoin, so a scar means “a major fall for this kind of asset.” Episodes are measured from the all-time high; a second crash from an interim high inside a still-open episode (Bitcoin in March 2020, the U.S. market in 1937–38) is a nested scar, measured from that interim high. Its angle follows the trough date and its length continues until the peak it fell from is reclaimed.

Families

Every specimen is assigned to exactly one family, the first it qualifies for in this order:

Old growth
A record spanning at least twenty-five calendar years.
Quiet compounders
Positive annualized growth with mean realized volatility in the calmest third of the collection.
Storm growers
Annualized growth in the top third of the collection, with volatility above the collection median.
Scarred survivors
At least two qualifying drawdown scars, or one still unrecovered.

Thresholds are terciles over the 97 featured specimens, so a family says how a tree compares with this collection, not with the whole market. A tree that qualifies for several families carries only the first.

Data provenance

The production artifact is baked from the Empty Data price lake. It contains derived annual returns, realized volatility, maximum drawdowns, recovery dates, and summary statistics—not the underlying daily price series. Coverage and latest observation dates vary by asset. Published annual and monthly returns are rounded to a whole percent, so the record cannot be inverted back into a provider's underlying closing prices; volatility, drawdown, and growth-rate statistics keep their full precision.

The interface reports the latest included price date globally and on each specimen. The committed artifact is validated before deployment.

Equity, fund, bond, commodity, and currency price history is provided by Twelve Data, whose daily record begins in 1970. Crypto market data includes information provided by CoinGecko. The century-long market and industry series are computed from the Kenneth R. French Data Library (daily Fama/French research factors and 12 Industry Portfolios, 1926 onward), compounded here into total-return indexes. Additional source-specific provenance is maintained in the upstream data lake.

Important limitations

  • Equity, fund, bond, commodity and currency series are dividend- and split-adjusted closes (total return), but the vendor’s dividend adjustment is thinner before 2013, so early growth for long-lived equities reads a little low (Coca-Cola since 1970 compounds about 1.5 points a year below a fully adjusted series). The century series are total-return indexes by construction. Crypto is a spot price with no yield. Each specimen states its basis under Data notes.
  • Partial calendar years are not directly comparable with complete years. A year counts as complete when it holds at least 60% of the asset’s own trading days (about 150 for market-hours instruments, 220 for crypto).
  • Annual returns run from the prior year’s last close to this year’s last close. Because published returns are rounded to a whole percent, accumulated-growth multiples on specimen pages are shown to two significant figures and are derived from the full-precision annualized growth rate over calendar time.
  • Featured crypto assets are those with the longest and most liquid records, which are also the ones that survived; the crypto long tail in the lake has a far weaker record than what the collection shows.
  • The universe is curated and survivor-biased; absent or delisted assets cannot appear.
  • Class-relative normalization improves legibility within an asset class but is not an absolute comparison of volatility between classes.
  • Historical performance is not predictive. Money Tree Forest is educational and is not investment advice.

Open methodology

The complete encoding contract is maintained with the source code. Visual encodings should change only when their corresponding data contract and documentation change together.

Privacy

Money Tree Forest does not use accounts, advertising trackers, or application cookies, and it does not collect user-submitted personal information. The hosting provider may process routine request metadata for security and delivery.
